At the beginning, Bakamo was just an idea. An idea that by exclusively analysing people's natural conversations across social media, research can glean better insights that lead to better decisions. While the theory sounded great, we did not know how it would work. Luckily we managed to convince a few clients to try. That's the official story.

## Behind the curtain

Behind the curtain we had to grapple with another problem: security. From early on, Bakamo sparked interest with a spectrum of clients, from fun consumer topics to serious health and societal issues. One day I got a call from a US foreign service official asking for details about a study that had been shared with them. The official said: wow, that's really impactful, we need to change what the ambassadors are talking about. I knew that we would need to change how Bakamo operates.

The concern was tangible. A few weeks later, a colleague found the windows of the office broken one morning, and the team freaked out. Did the government just try to break into our offices? I think it was the draught that broke the windows, but the episode shows how genuinely concerned the team was. The problem was that the Hungarian government had taken political control of the tax authorities and was using them to police companies, particularly those that "collaborated" with enemies of the state, such as human rights and advocacy organisations. Which we did, proudly.

## Late 2016

In late 2016 we incorporated Bakamo in the UK. The move paid off a few months later. Hungarian government media sites ran articles about a "UK private research group" whose "misleading" report aimed to confuse French voters on behalf of George Soros. They meant Bakamo's landmark report, [Patterns of Disinformation in the 2017 French Presidential Election](/research/french-election-2017), in which we exposed Russian disinformation tactics and their emotional hooks.

Over the years, we've grown accustomed to the power of natural conversation. We learnt that the insights are valuable precisely because they tell a different story from what some want the story to be. Even if it occasionally makes us paranoid.
