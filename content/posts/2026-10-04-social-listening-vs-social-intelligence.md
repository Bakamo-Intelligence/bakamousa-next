Both start from the same raw material: what people post in public. They differ in what they do with it, and in the kind of question they can answer.

## What social listening does

Social listening is monitoring. A tool collects mentions of a brand, a product or a keyword and reports how many there were, whether they were positive or negative, and how that changed since last week.

It answers questions such as: How much are we being talked about? Did the launch land? Is a complaint spreading? Communications and customer care teams need those answers quickly, and a dashboard gives them.

Its limit is built in. The tool looks for the terms you supplied, so it finds what you already knew to look for.

## What social intelligence does

Social intelligence is research. The question is not how often a brand is mentioned. It is how people make sense of a category, a condition or an issue when they talk to each other. Most of that conversation never mentions a brand at all.

The unit of analysis is meaning, not mentions. We look for the constructs people use to organise a decision, the tensions they carry, the language they reach for, and the things they have stopped arguing about.

That takes reading. At Bakamo, human analysts read the material. [The Reading Machine](/technology) maps the whole conversation into a semantic space, so the analyst can see where the volume sits and what lies at the edges. Interpretation stays with the analyst.

## The difference in short

- **Starting point.** Listening starts from keywords you choose. Intelligence starts from the conversation as it exists.
- **What is measured.** Listening counts mentions, sentiment and reach. Intelligence maps meanings and how they relate.
- **Sentiment.** For listening it is the main result. For intelligence it is a score applied after the fact, and [a complaint is often the most useful thing in the data](/blog/why-negativity-is-great-for-insight).
- **Output.** Listening gives you a dashboard. Intelligence gives you the architecture of a category: its constructs, tensions and vocabulary.
- **Use.** Listening tells you what happened. Intelligence tells you what to ask next, which is why we use it to [build surveys](/blog/survey-design-from-unprompted-conversation).

## Which one do you need?

If the question is "what is being said about us?", you need listening, and there are good tools for it.

If the question is "what should we be asking in the first place?", you need intelligence. That is the work [our method](/our-method) is built for.
